# 2026-09-18 UI / story polish

- Mobile: removed the virtual A button. The movement stick, camera drag and direct UI taps remain.
- Adventure menu: one Z-A-inspired shell for party, bag, Pok?dex, trainer, report and settings. Party art, localized names, HP and moves come from the profile. The Pok?dex uses national numbers and stays locked until Rowan awards it.
- New game: Rowan introduction and name entry, then the home television shot. No implicit Charmander, seen entry or debug inventory. Existing saved parties are preserved.
- Dialogue: ordinary conversations preserve the exploration camera. Large centered portraits sit behind the bottom dialogue band. TV broadcast gets its own held close shot.
- World: continuous, wider stream-bank slopes; reachable item placement; a finite physical story gate replaces the world-wide position clamp. Gate collision is created at runtime, so opening it does not leave a carved navigation hole.
- Flock staging: all Starly use connected dry-ground marks, then approach together on complete paths.
- Rendering: restrained bloom, saturation and exposure; removed chromatic aberration, film grain and routine world depth of field. Mobile and PC share grading, forward rendering and transparent shadow behavior, with resolution/shadow sampling differences retained for performance.
- Audio: empty/menu/interior biomes are silent for outdoor ambience and thunder.
- Authoring: `Tools/Level/templates/README.md` describes the new validated dry-route generator. `Tools/Story/README.md` covers story templates and the current chapter boundary.

Verification reports are archived in `previews`. Physical phone display and browser color-management differences have not been tested on a device. The template is for dry routes; caves, water geometry and outdoor seam connections still require authored design. The implemented story currently reaches the Route 202 capture lesson, not the rest of DP.

To publish a later version, run `DeployGame.exe` from this workspace. It builds through the connected Unity Editor, checks the WebGL player locally, publishes gh-pages, and checks the live site.
